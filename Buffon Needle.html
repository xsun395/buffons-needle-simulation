Crossing condition: $$x\leq \frac{L}{2}\sin\theta$$
For $L\leq d$: $$P(\text{cross})=\frac{2L}{\pi d}$$
Average over positions

The midpoint is uniformly distributed from a line, so $x$ is uniform on $[0,d/2]$.

Average over angles

The direction is uniform on $[0,\pi/2]$, and the average of $\sin\theta$ there is $2/\pi$. That angular average is where $\pi$ enters.

Put them together

The reachable distance is $\frac{L}{2}\sin\theta$. Averaging over both $x$ and $\theta$ gives $\frac{2L}{\pi d}$.

What to observe

At small $N$, one crossing can move $\hat{\pi}$ sharply. As $N$ grows, the curve wanders less and tends to settle near the reference line $\pi$. Each reset produces a different path.

A useful mistake

Long-needle challenge

The short-needle estimator assumes $L\leq d$. When $L>d$, some angles cross a line with probability 1, so the crossing probability is no longer linear in $L/d$.

$$P(\text{cross})=\frac{2}{\pi}\left[r-\sqrt{r^2-1}+\operatorname{arcsec}(r)\right],\qquad r=\frac{L}{d}>1$$

The inverse-trigonometric term is the missing correction.
